## Convergent validity: what it supports, and the one thing it flags

We administered MFQ-30 and PVQ-40 to the eleven-model panel and correlated each
model's neutral Reasoner axis positions against the two instruments' scores, testing
a sign pattern committed before the run. Ten of the thirteen administered predictions
held (four MFQ-2 predictions were not administered and are excluded). The result is
suggestive rather than decisive: with n = 11, no single correlation is individually
significant, and the value lies in the coherence of signs across many related metrics.
As an omnibus, ten correct signs out of thirteen is better than chance (binomial
p ≈ .05), though the predictions are not fully independent, so we read the pattern, not
the p-value. Model questionnaire responses also carry their own validity limits; the
decisive convergent test remains co-administration with the Reasoner in the human
confirmatory sample. With those caveats, the pass does its intended job — it tells us
which axes behave like their labels against external instruments, and which do not.

**Authority is the most strongly supported axis.** Its direct same-construct test — the
Reasoner Authority axis against the MFQ Authority foundation — is the largest single
correlation in the study (r = -0.63): models that reason skeptically about authority
score lower on the foundation that measures deference to it, exactly as predicted, and
the pattern is echoed by sanctity (-0.54) and the binding composite (-0.48). The two
predictions that failed were both against Schwartz values (conservation and
openness-to-change), and they failed in an informative way. Skepticism-of-authority in
these models does not travel along Schwartz's openness-conservation axis; it travels
with universalism (+0.65) and self-transcendence (+0.55). That is psychologically
coherent — an LLM that insists authority be justified tends to do so in the name of
fairness and protection of the weak, not in the name of novelty or self-direction. The
miss is in the bridging assumption (that "skeptical" should look like Schwartz
"openness"), not in the axis, which passed its direct test decisively.

**Obligation Scope is the cleanest.** All four pre-registered predictions held:
universal-scope reasoning tracks universalism (+0.43), self-transcendence (+0.52), and
benevolence (+0.59), and inversely tracks parochial loyalty (-0.38). The axis places
models on exactly the individualizing/universalizing gradient its label describes.

**Moral Agent is supported, modestly.** All three predictions carried the right sign but
at smaller magnitudes (self-direction +0.25, openness-to-change +0.36, binding -0.19).
The reassurance is in the breadth of the pattern rather than any one coefficient: the
autonomous pole associates positively with the agentic, self-focused values
(achievement, hedonism, stimulation) and negatively with the relational and communal
ones (care, tradition, conformity, loyalty), which is what "the individual, not the
person-in-relationship, is the moral unit" should look like. At n = 11 the small
coefficients are expected; the sign-consistency across a dozen related metrics is the
signal.

**Moral Domain is the axis this pass does not validate, and we report that plainly.**
The construct is domain *breadth* — whether morality extends beyond harm and fairness to
purity, loyalty, and duty — so the discriminating external test is the MFQ *binding*
foundations, the ones that are "in" for a broad domain and "out" for a narrow one. The
Domain axis's correlation with the binding composite is -0.06: effectively zero. The
axis instead correlates strongly and negatively with the *individualizing* foundations
(care -0.77, fairness -0.59), and the relative contrast that might have reconciled this,
individualizing-minus-binding, is also wrong-signed (-0.39). This is not a general
method artifact: the Domain axis is no more confounded with overall MFQ endorsement
(-0.32) than Authority is (-0.40), and Authority validated. What the model panel shows,
then, is that Domain returns a null on the test that would confirm it and an anomaly on
the one that would not — the panel neither confirms nor cleanly interprets the axis
against the MFQ. The most likely explanations are two, and they are separable in the
confirmatory study: either the Domain scenarios are being scored in a direction that
does not match the intended breadth polarity, or "domain breadth" as the Reasoner
operationalizes it genuinely diverges from MFQ foundation endorsement (for instance,
because the axis partly indexes how much a model moralizes harm and fairness rather than
whether it admits additional foundations). We do not resolve that here; we flag it as the
priority to pin down — by auditing the Domain scenarios' scoring against the construct,
and by testing Domain directly against a binding-versus-individualizing contrast in the
human sample — before Moral Domain is treated as a validated dimension.

Taken together, the pass leaves the four-dimension structure in good standing: two axes
(Authority, Scope) are supported by external instruments, one (Moral Agent) is supported
modestly, and one (Moral Domain) is flagged for construct clarification rather than
confirmed. That distribution — most of the structure corroborated, one component
surfaced for repair before it reaches human data — is precisely the outcome an
exploratory, pre-registered convergent-validity pass exists to produce.
