# Model vs. human profiles on MFQ-30, MFQ-2, and PVQ-40

How the eleven-model panel's self-report on the three established instruments compares to
published human norms. **Model numbers are ours (exact, averaged across 5 iterations per
model, n = 11 models).** Human numbers are published normative values, shown as approximate
and **to be finalized against the source tables** before publication. Two caveats frame
everything below: (1) our pilot humans never took these questionnaires — they answered only
the 12 Reasoner scenarios — so the comparison is model-vs-published-norms, not against our own
sample; and (2) a model rating "how much does this describe you" is trained-persona output,
not a psychology, so this is descriptive, not an inference about a population.

**Headline:** on all three instruments the models resemble an *exaggerated WEIRD-secular-liberal
human* — care/individualizing-dominant, merit-embracing but equality-skeptical, and strikingly
low on the binding / purity / tradition cluster — and they cluster far more tightly than any
human population.

## MFQ-30 (0–5)

| Foundation | Models | Humans (US, ≈) |
|---|--:|--:|
| Care | 4.28 | ~3.6 |
| Fairness | 4.51 | ~3.7 |
| Loyalty | 2.32 | ~2.9 |
| Authority | 2.02 | ~2.9 |
| Sanctity | 1.34 | ~2.6 |
| *Individualizing* | 4.39 | ~3.65 |
| *Binding* | 1.89 | ~2.8 |

The individualizing–binding gap is **2.5 in the models vs ~0.85 in the general US sample**
(~1.5 even among liberal humans). The models sit *beyond* the most liberal human subgroup:
individualizing near ceiling, sanctity near floor.

## MFQ-2 (1–5)

| Foundation | Models | Humans (≈) |
|---|--:|--:|
| Care | 4.54 | ~3.7 |
| Equality | 2.02 | ~2.9 |
| Proportionality | 4.24 | ~3.7 |
| Loyalty | 3.13 | ~2.8 |
| Authority | 3.09 | ~3.1 |
| Purity | 1.90 | ~2.7 |

The split-fairness result is the informative part: models *embrace proportionality* (merit, 4.24)
while *rejecting equality* (equal outcomes, 2.02) — a meritocratic-care profile — and sit well
below humans on purity. That equality/proportionality split is why the pre-registered
`scope × equality` prediction flipped sign (universal moral concern is not economic egalitarianism).

## PVQ-40 (mean-centered value hierarchy)

Model hierarchy, high to low: self-direction (+1.28), universalism (+1.20), benevolence (+1.14),
conformity (~0.00), security (−0.19), stimulation (−0.26), hedonism (−0.45), achievement (−0.98),
tradition (−0.98), power (−1.99). Schwartz's pan-cultural human hierarchy agrees at the extremes
— benevolence, self-direction, and universalism at the top; **power always lowest** — but humans
place the *conservation* values (security, conformity, tradition) in the upper-middle, whereas
the models push them to the center or below. So it is the human hierarchy with the conservation
block suppressed and self-direction/universalism elevated.

## Dispersion (the compression theme, again)

The model foundation SDs are small (MFQ-30 foundations 0.28–0.52; MFQ-2 0.29–0.45; PVQ values
0.23–0.89) and the eleven models cluster tightly. They do not span the human range; they occupy
a narrow band inside its liberal-secular corner — the same compression the Reasoner axes show at
neutral framing, now corroborated on three independent, decades-validated instruments.

## Why this matters for the Reasoner

Atari et al.'s "Beyond the WEIRD" point is that non-WEIRD human samples weight binding, purity,
and loyalty far more heavily than WEIRD ones. The models resemble WEIRD-liberal humans
specifically, so measured against the full global human range their default moral self-report is
parked at one culturally-specific extreme. That is independent corroboration — through a
different instrument family — of the gap the Reasoner is built to measure: an unframed default
that is both compressed and culturally narrow.

## Next: cultural perspective-taking under framing

An open extension (see conversation): re-administer the three instruments under a **society
framing** — "answer as a typical person living in [country]" — for countries with published
human norms (Atari's ~25-country MFQ-2 set; ESS PVQ; Schwartz country scores). This tests two
things at once: (1) whether framing *de-compresses* the models on these instruments the way it
does on the Reasoner axes, and (2) whether the model's culture-simulated profile *matches the
real human norm* for that country — an accuracy test of cultural perspective-taking, and a probe
of whether the WEIRD default reflects inability or merely an unframed default. Cheap to run
(same 55–165-call structure per framing condition).
