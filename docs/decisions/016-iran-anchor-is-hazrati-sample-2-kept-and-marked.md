# 016 - Iran's reference mean is Hazrati sample 2, kept and marked

**Date:** 2026-09-07
**Status:** active

Decided 2026-09-07, logged 2026-09-08.

## The decision

Iran's reference-sample mean is Hazrati, Nejat and Daneshi (2025) sample 2, binding composite
3.333 on the 1-to-5 scale after the +1 shift from their 0-to-4 administration. It is kept, not
dropped. It is marked `[*]` in every place the anchor enters: B3's two tables, B6's measured row,
and the Iran block in B4, which carries the source, the sample's own caveats, and the overshoot
under all three anchors the source offers. The viewer says the same on its first tab and on the
Farsi chart. It is never pooled into `mfq2_country_means.csv`.

## Why

Iran is the only Farsi country and the only one not in Atari et al. (2023) Study 2, so it carries
the whole Farsi arm, and its English-framed overshoot, +1.244, is the largest in the grid.

The objection that held this open was that a settled rule barred it. Read at source,
`reasoner-study` decision 1 bars pooling independent validation means into the reference file,
and names Iran as an example of what must not be pooled. The reference file here has never held
Iran; the anchor lives in its own file with its own citation. The rule was satisfied all along.
What remained was the real question: a separately sourced, differently sampled anchor, or no
Iran at all.

Kept, because the source is a validation of the same official translation we administered, its
sample composition is stated and can be disclosed, and the choice among its anchors is
conservative. Sample 2 is the largest of the three values the source supports, 3.333 against
sample 1's 3.231 and the n-weighted pool's 3.304, so the overshoot reported everywhere is the
smallest of the three. The sign and the ordering of the Iran result do not depend on the choice;
the magnitude does, by up to 0.102, and the table shows it.

## Rejected alternatives

**Drop Iran and report its panel values uncharted alongside India, Sweden and the United
States.** Rejected: a usable anchor exists, and deleting the Farsi arm's only comparison because
its sample is imperfect loses more than disclosing the imperfection.

**The n-weighted pool, 3.304, or sample 1, 3.231.** Rejected: both raise the overshoot. Choosing
the anchor that enlarges the finding would need a reason beyond preferring the larger number,
and the pool mixes two collections the authors report separately.

**Amend the settled rule.** Unnecessary once the rule was read rather than summarised.

## Consequences and gotchas

The `[*]` marker points at a caveat and the `[d12]` marker at a decision; the two conventions
coexist in B3 and are footnoted there.

Iran's person-level SD now comes from the authors' shared respondent-level files (issue #21),
computed over their own composite columns: sample 2, n=989, SD 0.802. That gives Iran a
reference-sample SE and the paper's human-SD table its Farsi row.

Read the rule, not a review's account of it. This decision was carried as unresolved through the
2026-09-05 and 2026-09-07 handoffs and three session reports on the strength of a reviewer's
summary of decision 1 that was narrower than the text, and after it was decided it was listed as
open once more by mistake. The record of that is in `reviews/astra-paper-review-2026-09-08.md`
and the 2026-09-08 handoff.
