# 018 - Morocco is reported under Spanish throughout; the Arabic arm is data, not a comparison

**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Status:** active

## The decision

Morocco belongs to the Spanish group in every view: the country tables, the ordering, the
distances in human standard deviations, and the six-language framing average, where it counts
under Spanish and not Arabic. Its comparisons against the human mean use the Spanish arm. The
Arabic-framed Morocco cells stay in the data and appear where every condition is listed, B3a, B6
and B4's within-Arabic framing block, and no number compares the Arabic arm to the Spanish arm
or to the human mean.

## Why

Atari et al. administered Morocco's sample in Spanish. A panel framed in Arabic set against a
mean collected in Spanish is a language mismatch inside a study whose point is administration
language, and every review round since the split raised it. Reporting Morocco in the language
its reference sample answered in removes the mismatch instead of marking it.

## Rejected alternatives

Keeping Morocco in the Arabic group with a marked row, decision 12. It preserved a four-country
Arabic ordering at the cost of a comparison the design does not license, and it drew a finding
in every round.

Comparing the two arms against each other as a check on the mismatch, as the paper's Limits did.
The two framed means differ by 0.007 while the two unframed means differ by 0.324, so the arms'
framing contrasts differ substantially and the point comparison said less than it appeared to.

## Consequences and gotchas

The Arabic ordering rests on three countries. The Spanish ordering has six. The six-language
framing average moves, because Morocco's +1.809 Spanish-arm effect now sits in the Spanish mean
and its +1.478 Arabic-arm effect leaves the Arabic mean; every dependent number is listed in the
commit that applies this. The viewer follows the paper.

## Supersedes / amends

Supersedes decision 12's grouping clause, "Morocco stays in the Arabic group for the ordering and
foundation-shift views". Decision 12's anchor clause, compared on the Spanish arm, carries into
this entry. Both runs remain in the data, as decision 12 also required.
