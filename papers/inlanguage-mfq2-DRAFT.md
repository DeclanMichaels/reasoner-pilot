# The Language Carries the Population; the Prompt Carries the Caricature

## Summary

Our society-framing study left a confound standing: when a model told to answer "as a typical person from Egypt" overshot the real Egyptian mean, we could not say whether the miss came from what the model produces about Egyptians or from the English it was asked in, because the human norms were collected in Arabic. Here we close that gap, and closing it split the phenomenon in two. We administered the official Arabic, Japanese, and Farsi translations of the MFQ-2, the same instrument behind the published country norms, to the same 11-model panel, each language in two conditions: framed, where the model answers as a typical person from Egypt, Japan, or Iran with the instruction given in that language, and neutral, no system prompt at all, just the questionnaire in the language. The neutral in-language condition is the new instrument: it separates answering in Arabic from answering as an Egyptian. The caricature survives translation untouched. Framed in Arabic, the panel lands exactly where it lands framed in English, 4.61 on the binding composite against a human 4.27, with any language effect bounded below 0.04 points. But the unframed default turns out to be language-indexed, and what a language carries varies from everything to nothing. Presented the questionnaire in Japanese with no framing at all, the panel lands on the measured Japanese population mean, 2.66 against 2.65, on the very population the framed condition distorts most. Arabic moves the default a third of a point toward binding and no further. Farsi moves it nowhere: the Farsi default is the English default to within 0.02. And Iran is where the template fails hardest: framed in English the panel renders Iran as Egypt, 4.59 against 4.61, while the two measured populations sit nearly a point apart, an overshoot of +1.26, the largest we have measured on any country. Framing in Farsi claws back 0.23 of it and leaves a full point standing. The two layers are dissociable, and for Japan they pull in opposite directions: the language alone puts the panel on the population, and the instruction to roleplay the population pulls it 0.80 away. Telling a model to be Japanese makes it less accurate than speaking Japanese to it.

## The design

The MFQ-2 (Atari et al., 2023) rates 36 statements 1 to 5 across six foundations; the binding composite averages Loyalty, Authority, and Purity, the foundations that separate traditional from secular-liberal moral profiles. We used the official translations distributed with the instrument's own validation materials, so the models answered the same Arabic, Japanese, and Farsi wordings the human norms were built on; the keying was confirmed against the scoring block included in the Farsi file. The panel is the pilot's eleven models from eight labs. Five iterations per cell, item order freshly randomized every run (verified afterward: 770 recorded runs across this and the baseline studies, 770 distinct presentation orders), ratings returned keyed to items, so order cannot touch a score. English framed and neutral baselines come from the prior run under the identical protocol. Human anchors: Egypt 4.27 and Japan 2.65 from the instrument's 19-nation validation; Iran 3.33 from an independent Iranian validation of the Persian MFQ-2 (N = 989), rescaled from its 0-4 administration, a convenience sample that skews young, online, and female, which we carry as a caveat on every Iran number. The unit of analysis is the model throughout: eleven clusters, aggregated before testing, bootstrap intervals over models, exact sign-flip permutation on paired differences, ten tests in one Holm-corrected family. Nothing was pre-registered; every test is exploratory and labeled so.

## The results

Binding composite, panel mean over eleven models, against each population's measured mean:

| | human | framed, English | framed, in-language | neutral, in-language | neutral, English |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Egypt / Arabic | 4.27 | 4.61 | 4.61 | 3.03 | 2.71 |
| Japan / Japanese | 2.65 | 3.67 | 3.46 | 2.66 | 2.71 |
| Iran / Farsi | 3.33 | 4.59 | 4.36 | 2.72 | 2.71 |

**The caricature survives translation.** Framed as an Egyptian in Arabic, the panel overshoots the Egyptian mean by +0.34 (95% CI +0.25 to +0.43), and the per-model difference between the English and Arabic framed conditions is -0.006, bounded within 0.04 points; since the English cells were collected three days earlier, that bound doubles as a stability check across collection dates. Japan's overshoot shrinks under Japanese administration, +1.02 to +0.81 (the paired reduction of 0.21 is real, CI +0.08 to +0.36), and persists. In every framed condition the panel collapses into a shared portrait, between-model SD 0.15 to 0.22, at or below its neutral disagreement: the same shared-caricature signature the English run found, now reproduced in three scripts.

**The default is language-indexed, and unevenly.** With no framing at all, the language of the questionnaire moves the panel's resting point. Japanese moves it onto the measured Japanese mean: 2.66 against 2.65, the displacement bounded within a fifth of a scale point at 95 percent confidence, on the lowest-endorsing population in the reference dataset. Arabic moves it a third of a point toward binding (+0.33, CI +0.21 to +0.46) and stops, a quarter of the way to the Egyptian mean, with the panel's widest disagreement about where the Arabic default even is. Farsi moves it nowhere: 2.72 against the English 2.71, any carried signal bounded below 0.17. One language contains its population, one carries a trace, one carries nothing, and nothing in the framed conditions would have told you which was which.

**The template is one portrait; the populations are three.** Framed in English, Iran and Egypt are the same country to this panel: 4.59 and 4.61. The measured populations sit 0.94 apart, because the Iranian sample is not a generic high-binding religious society; it is proportionality-highest, equality-lowest, with authority near the scale midpoint. The panel's Iran error concentrates exactly where the template predicts and the population departs: Authority rendered 4.48 against a measured 3.05, Loyalty 4.45 against 3.63, Purity 4.14 against 3.32, while Equality, where the population happens to match the template's assumption, is accurate to two hundredths. The overshoot is +1.26 (CI +1.15 to +1.35), every model overshoots, and removing any single model never brings the panel below +1.23. Framing in Farsi rather than English reduces it by 0.23 (CI +0.14 to +0.32), the Japanese shrink replicated in a second country, and still leaves +1.03 standing.

**The dissociation.** Framing displaces every panel strongly, +1.6 in Arabic, +0.8 in Japanese, +1.6 in Farsi relative to each language's own neutral, regardless of how much population signal the language carried. So the two behaviors come apart: what the language of administration elicits tracks the population well, partially, or not at all, and what the framing instruction elicits is the same tradition-template everywhere. For Japan they oppose each other. The distortion our society-framing study called the largest in that dataset is not an absence of population-consistent behavior; under Japanese administration the panel produces that behavior unprompted, and the explicit instruction to produce it moves the panel away from it.

## What the refusal looks like

One cell in 330 produced no rating, and it was not an extraction failure. On one specific item-order draw of the Arabic neutral condition, Kimi declines the questionnaire outright, in Arabic: as an AI model it has no opinions, feelings, or human self the statements could truthfully describe, so it cannot fill in a self-report scale. Under the fixed-seed protocol every retry replays the same draw, and the refusal reproduces deterministically, four attempts, while the same model answers the other four orders of the same condition without complaint. We report the cell as a refusal and score that condition on four iterations. A refusal that appears in one language and one item order of an unframed questionnaire, from a model that completes the same instrument in English every time, is a small behavior worth a line of its own: whatever triggers it is not the instrument.

## Why this matters

Cultural adaptation by prompt and cultural behavior by language are different capabilities, and evaluations that test only one will misread deployed systems. A benchmark that scores framed behavior measures the template; the template is shared across the industry, uniform across countries, and wrong in proportion to how far a real population has drifted from the stereotype of its region. A benchmark that scores only English behavior misses what the same model does under local-language administration entirely, and that behavior can be dramatically better, as with Japanese, or no better at all, as with Farsi. The deployment reading is blunt: an assistant serving a population in its own language may represent that population better unframed than the same assistant instructed to adopt the culture, and which of those is true for a given language cannot be predicted from the framed behavior. For the audit conversation this adds a second independent argument against reading a model's cultural competence off its persona behavior: the persona is a costume, and for at least one population the model wears the costume over clothes that already fit.

## Limitations

Eleven models is a panel, not a sample; inferential statements are about this panel, and nothing here was pre-registered, so every test is exploratory however small its corrected p. One instrument, three languages, one framed country per language: whether Arabic neutral would track Gulf, Levantine, or North African means differently is unmeasured, and Arabic cannot be assigned to a single national population at all, which may itself explain its partial carry. Human anchors are single studies per country; the Iran anchor comes from a different research group than the Egypt and Japan anchors, on a shifted response scale we rescaled linearly, from a young, online, roughly 70 percent female sample, so the true Iranian population mean is plausibly higher than 3.33 and the Iran overshoot somewhat smaller than +1.26, though a 1.4-point Authority error does not plausibly reduce to sampling. The framing-instruction translations are ours, not validated; the English-Arabic identity on Egypt argues the framed conditions are not translation artifacts, but does not prove it for Farsi or Japanese. The English-framed Iran arm was added after an audit of the first draft named its absence as the design's open hole, the family's only analysis whose motivation postdates seeing other results. Models have plausibly seen the MFQ-2 items and its published norms in training, so all of this is steerability and behavioral repertoire, not cultural understanding; the Japanese neutral result in particular is consistent with both an internalized population profile and memorized questionnaire norms surfacing under Japanese text, and distinguishing those requires an instrument the models have not seen. That instrument is what the confirmatory study exists to build.

## What comes next

The confirmatory Reasoner design gains a condition: in-language administration alongside neutral and framed, on expert-authored scenarios that carry no published norms to memorize. The scenario bank for that study is built and model-checked; its human side, in-group samples across five traditions, is the study. If the language-carries-the-population effect survives on an instrument that cannot be retrieved from training data, it stops being a memorization curiosity and becomes a deployment principle.
